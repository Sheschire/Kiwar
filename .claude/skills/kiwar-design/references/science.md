# Verified bibliography (checked 23 October 2026)

Every entry was checked against Crossref/OpenAlex metadata, the publisher page, PubMed Central or
an author repository. Evidence levels:

- **[EXP]** controlled experiment (causal, within the tested conditions);
- **[CORR]** survey or observational data (no causal inference);
- **[QUAL]** analysis of games or interviews (taxonomy, grounded theory);
- **[REV]** literature review; **[THEORY]** explanatory model or essay.

Corrections to the brief the owner received from ChatGPT are flagged **Correction**.

## Motivation and needs

**Ryan, R. M., Rigby, C. S. & Przybylski, A. K. (2006).** The Motivational Pull of Video Games: A
Self-Determination Theory Approach. _Motivation and Emotion_ 30(4), 344–360.
doi:10.1007/s11031-006-9051-8 — [EXP + CORR + THEORY]

- Four studies: three lab studies with students (n = 89, 50, 58), then an online survey of 730
  MMO players.
- In-game autonomy and competence go with enjoyment, preference and well-being change. Only
  study 4 tests relatedness, which predicts enjoyment and future play independently.
- **Correction:** pages are 344–360 (Crossref), not 347–363.
- **Kiwar:** the player must see the effect of their choices and choose between viable
  strategies. More actions is not more autonomy, and more numbers is not more competence.

**Ballou, N., Denisova, A., Ryan, R. M., Rigby, C. S. & Deterding, S. (2024).** The Basic Needs in
Games Scale (BANGS): A new tool for investigating positive and negative video game experiences.
_International Journal of Human-Computer Studies_ 188, 103289. doi:10.1016/j.ijhcs.2024.103289 —
measurement tool

- Five studies, 1,246 participants.
- Six subscales cover satisfaction and frustration of autonomy, competence and relatedness.
- Autonomy frustration is the least reliable subscale.
- **Correction:** this is a journal article, not a CHI '24 paper.
- **Kiwar:** an optional questionnaire for playtests with the owner's friends, to measure
  perceived autonomy and competence instead of inferring them from play time.

## Idle games

**Alharthi, S. A., Alsaedi, O., Toups Dugas, P. O., Tanenbaum, T. J. & Hammer, J. (2018).**
Playing to Wait: A Taxonomy of Idle Games. _CHI '18_, paper 621. doi:10.1145/3173574.3174195 —
[QUAL]

- Grounded-theory analysis of 66 idle games, plus 10 non-idle games to set the genre boundary.
- Idle games move players "from playing to planning". They mix rules that let the player leave
  with rules that reward coming back.
- A study of games, not of players.
- **Kiwar:** map every phase onto act → invest → wait → return → reinvest. The wait has to end
  in a decision.

**Cutting, J., Gundry, D. & Cairns, P. (2019).** Busy doing nothing? What do players do in idle
games? _IJHCS_ 122, 133–144. doi:10.1016/j.ijhcs.2018.09.006 — [CORR]

- Survey of 1,972 Neko Atsume players.
- Four engagement factors: time playing, direct sociability, social-media sociability and
  checking frequency. They fit existing engagement models poorly.
- **Correction:** "engagement as a habit" is proposed for future studies; it was not tested.
- **Kiwar:** what matters is the value felt on return, not session length. One game,
  self-reported data.

**Fizek, S. (2018).** Interpassivity and the Joy of Delegated Play in Idle Games. _Transactions of
the DiGRA_ 3(3). doi:10.26503/todigra.v3i3.81 — [THEORY]

- Idle games play themselves, so the player's participation becomes optional.
- **Kiwar:** this backs charter item 9. Watching the village run is part of the pleasure, which
  is why kiwis must live in place.

**Deterding, S. (2016).** Progress Wars: Idle Games and the Demarcation of "Real" Games.
_DiGRA/FDG 2016_ abstract proceedings. doi:10.26503/dl.v2016i2.851 — [THEORY]

- Idle games began as parodies of grinding and progress bars.
- **Kiwar:** progress alone is not a game. Each step must open a choice.

## Difficulty, success and balance

**Brändle, F., Wu, C. M. & Schulz, E. (2025).** Leveling up fun: learning progress, expectations,
and success influence enjoyment in video games. _Scientific Reports_ 15, 34153.
doi:10.1038/s41598-025-14628-2 — [CORR + EXP]

- **Data:** levels and votes from Robozzle (7,994 levels), Trackmania (138,662) and Super Mario
  Maker (115,032), plus two online experiments (n = 98 analysed, n = 44).
- **Inverted U:** intermediate difficulty is preferred.
- **Expectations:** a match between expected and actual difficulty is preferred.
- **Points:** higher point values raise engagement (behaviour) even without any real reward.
- **Limits:** learning progress was simulated, not measured. In the game data, success cannot be
  separated from difficulty.
- **Kiwar:** keep expectations and obstacles consistent (forecasts, announced threats). Visible
  big numbers on collection support engagement, not necessarily lasting fun.

**Cutting, J., Deterding, S., Demediuk, S. & Sephton, N. (2023).** Difficulty-skill balance does
not affect engagement and enjoyment: a pre-registered study using artificial
intelligence-controlled difficulty. _Royal Society Open Science_ 10(2), 220274.
doi:10.1098/rsos.220274 — [EXP]

- 311 players analysed, against an MCTS opponent tuned to easy, balanced or hard.
- No significant effect on enjoyment or behaviour.

**Deterding, S. & Cutting, J. (2023).** Objective Difficulty-Skill Balance Impacts Perceived
Balance but Not Behaviour: A Test of Flow and Self-Determination Theory Predictions. _PACM HCI_
7(CHI PLAY), 1179–1205. doi:10.1145/3611065 — [EXP]

- n = 309. Balance changes how balanced the game feels, not what players do.
- The authors point to appraisals, **novelty and progress** instead.
- **Kiwar:** this is why charter item 2 puts new content per age before fine tuning.

**Larche, C. J. & Dixon, M. J. (2020).** The relationship between the skill-challenge balance,
game expertise, flow and the urge to keep playing complex mobile games. _Journal of Behavioral
Addictions_ 9(3), 606–616. doi:10.1556/2006.2020.00070 — [EXP]

- 60 experienced Candy Crush players.
- At-level and hard games give similar flow and urge to continue, both above easy games.
- **Checked:** hard games are clearly more frustrating (easy 1.73, at level 3.65, hard 4.27).
- The paper belongs to the addiction literature.
- **Kiwar:** a hard landing can keep players going _and_ frustrate them. Pair difficulty with
  information and a way back (the retreat, repairable losses). The urge to continue is not a
  measure of fun.

**Guo, Z., Thawonmas, R. & Ren, X. (2024).** Rethinking dynamic difficulty adjustment for video
game design. _Entertainment Computing_ 50, 100663. doi:10.1016/j.entcom.2024.100663 — [REV]

- The effects of dynamic difficulty adjustment (DDA) on players are mixed, and DDA leans too
  heavily on Flow theory.
- The authors propose a goal-based framework in 6 steps.
- **Kiwar:** this backs charter item 7. The aliens follow a world clock, never a hidden
  per-player difficulty.

## Goals and progress

**Kivetz, R., Urminsky, O. & Zheng, Y. (2006).** The Goal-Gradient Hypothesis Resurrected.
_Journal of Marketing Research_ 43(1), 39–58. doi:10.1509/jmkr.43.1.39 — [EXP + field]

- Effort accelerates as the goal gets closer. A 12-stamp card with 2 stamps given fills faster
  than a plain 10-stamp card.

**Nunes, J. C. & Drèze, X. (2006).** The Endowed Progress Effect. _Journal of Consumer Research_
32(4), 504–512. doi:10.1086/500480 — [EXP]

- Progress already granted toward a goal raises completion.
- ⚠️ the popular car-wash percentages were not checked.

**Teng, C.-I., Shiau, W.-L., Cheng, T. C. E. & Huang, H.-Y. (2022).** Drawing goals nearer: Using
the goal-gradient perspective to increase online game usage. _International Journal of
Information Management_ 66, 102522. doi:10.1016/j.ijinfomgt.2022.102522 — [CORR]

- 1,724 online gamers, cross-sectional data.
- Expected character growth and self-efficacy go with perceived goal proximity and motivation.
- Its stated aim (raise usage) is not one of ours.
- **Kiwar, for these three:** objectives per age with a visible next step (chantier 31), and
  progress toward the next age always on screen. No fake progress bars everywhere.

## Loss, risk and permadeath

**Kahneman, D. & Tversky, A. (1979).** Prospect Theory: An Analysis of Decision under Risk.
_Econometrica_ 47(2), 263–291. doi:10.2307/1914185 — [EXP + THEORY]

- Losses weigh more than equal gains, and low probabilities are overweighted.
- **Kiwar:** losing a veteran kiwi weighs a lot. Show the risk honestly before the launch.

**Carter, M., Gibbs, M. & Wadley, G. (2013).** Death and dying in DayZ. _IE '13_, 1–6.
doi:10.1145/2513002.2513013 — [QUAL]

- A costly death intensifies social interaction and investment, and creates moral dilemmas.

**Allison, F., Carter, M. & Gibbs, M. (2015).** Good Frustrations: The Paradoxical Pleasure of
Fearing Death in DayZ. _OzCHI '15_, 119–123. doi:10.1145/2838739.2838810 — [CORR]

- 1,704 survey responses. Losing weeks of progress frustrates, yet most players see permadeath as
  central to the appeal.
- **Kiwar:** permadeath on landings is fine if the player chose the risk. Keep the memorial and
  the veterans, so that loss and survival both carry meaning.

**Copcic, A., McKenzie, S. & Hobbs, M. (2013).** Permadeath: A review of literature. _IEEE IGIC
2013_, 40–47. doi:10.1109/IGIC.2013.6659156 — [REV]

- **Correction:** this is a literature review, not a DayZ study.

## Frameworks

- **Hunicke, R., LeBlanc, M. & Zubek, R. (2004).** MDA: A Formal Approach to Game Design and Game
  Research. AAAI-04 Workshop on Challenges in Game AI (WS-04-04), no DOI.
  - Mechanics → dynamics → aesthetics. Design from the feeling wanted back to the rule.
- **Csikszentmihalyi (flow)**: a frame for questions, never a formula for wait times.

## Research policy

For any new reference record: exact title, authors, year, venue, volume/pages, DOI, study type,
sample, result, limits, link, and how far it transfers to Kiwar. Never cite from memory alone;
mark unverified details with ⚠️.
